# Claim Audit and corrections

Adapted from the existing System/Claim-Audit and error-log mechanism. Audit important chapter/topic conclusions, reusable concepts, publication drafts, new counterevidence or disputed AI claims. Ordinary discussion needs only proportionate checks.

1. Identify scope, note/edition, claim ID and exact disputed wording. Mark a paraphrase as the auditor's paraphrase. A possible misuse is a risk probe, not something the original reader actually asserted.
2. Reopen the original materials. Check source existence, locator, faithful excerpt and support for the exact claim; distinguish attribution from truth. Verify OCR quotations against images. Record actual access and unread portions.
3. Check theory/fact, author/consensus, correlation/causation and group/individual boundaries. Statistics require measurement, population, comparisons, timing and available uncertainty.
4. Consider relevant counterevidence or competing interpretations and conditions that would change the conclusion. If evidence is unavailable, say so instead of manufacturing a pass. Independent review can help when authorized, but never substitutes for sources.
5. Record result and correction/dependency actions in the session or audit note. Review only important claims; do not convert each audit item into a reader test.

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| pass / green | All important in-scope claims were actually checked and are supported within stated boundaries; not certainty forever |
| caution / amber | Support is partial, disputed or access-limited; preserve with limitations |
| block / red | Fabrication, unsupported critical claim, invalid inference or diagnosis; retract/correct or isolate as a question |
| not_run | No completed audit; never an implicit pass |

An unresolved blocked claim makes the note block; partial coverage cannot yield overall pass. A well-labeled hypothesis or ordinary personal record is not automatically blocked for lacking empirical support. Record dates only for actual verification; editing is not verification.

When corrected, recheck the source, explain the revised reading and preserve the original statement/history. In the reader's `AI-ERROR-LOG.md` (or existing equivalent), append: ID/discovery date, erroneous wording and locator, error type, discovery method, correction and actual basis, possible cause explicitly marked speculative, affected notes/actions, remaining uncertainty and relevant regression case. Search relevant dependents and update/retract misuse; do not silently erase history. Read unresolved/relevant prior errors at recovery so confirmed mistakes are less likely to recur.

Maintain important claims inline with IDs. A separate `CLAIM-REGISTRY.md` is optional for larger libraries, not an extra V0.1 dependency. The log records real discovered errors, never hypothetical incidents as actual history.
